# FAR 15 and 16 Quick Reference

> [!note]
> This is a proposal operator quick sheet, not a clause digest. Use it to decide how to read the buying move in front of you.

## Signals that matter early

- FAR 15 style source-selection language usually means evaluation logic, strengths, weaknesses, and discriminator framing need to be explicit.
- FAR 16 ordering language often shifts attention to ordering mechanics, task-order scope control, and vehicle-fit friction.
- If the buyer emphasizes best value but gives sparse tradeoff detail, assume the narrative has to make discriminators easy to score.
- If the structure points toward labor-hour or T and M risk, price realism and staffing logic need to show up earlier in capture.

## What to extract from the solicitation

1. Evaluation vocabulary the customer repeats.
2. Contract and order structure that changes pricing posture.
3. Attachments or workload tables that imply hidden staffing assumptions.
4. Any language that narrows the real competitive set.

## Common operator mistakes

- Treating every RFP like a neutral compliance exercise.
- Missing how vehicle structure changes teaming, pricing, or incumbent advantage.
- Waiting until Red Team to reconcile Section L / M logic.
